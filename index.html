THE WIZARD’S HANDBOOK

Fire meets Water

Share one screen locally, or choose Play on two screens to create or join a hosted room. Each wizard has their own hand, spell tower, and discard pile.

  • Start with 100 HP, 3 Mana, and up to 5 spell slabs, drawing as many as your deck contains. Decks may contain 1–30 slabs. Caps are 100 HP, 10 Mana, and 30 Shield. Shield, Embers/Flow, Loaded and Enhancement start at zero, with no active Aura.
  • Each wizard rolls one opening die. The higher roll takes the first turn; ties make both wizards roll again. Opening rolls use no Fate or Loaded bonuses.
  • The wizard going second draws one extra slab at the beginning of their first turn if their tower has a slab, normally starting that turn with 6 rather than 5. Slabs used for defending Fate before that turn remain spent. This opening draw happens once and grants no extra mana.
  • Both wizards take one turn per round. Each gains 2 Mana at the start of their own turn after their opening turn. Unspent Mana carries over up to 10.
  • After each wizard’s opening turn, draw up to 6 slabs at the beginning of their turn. Draws stop when the tower is empty. A full hand uses three slabs on each row.
  • Use one main action per own turn: cast a primary spell, attempt Enhancement, or activate Aura. Fire and Water slabs are primary spells. Cast Spell pays the printed Mana cost. Ember spending choices are made before casting. Fire-7 and Fire-14 spend all Embers automatically.
  • Fire-0 costs 0 Mana and gains 1 Ember immediately, up to 5. Fire-15 costs 0 Mana and immediately gains 1 Ember and 1 Mana, capped at 5 and 10 independently. Both use your primary action without rolling dice or opening Fate. Other current primary spells roll 2d6 before Fate. The dice and eligible Loaded bonus are displayed first. Select one eligible Fate slab, then Cast Fate plays it and gives the other player a response. Skip Fate passes without playing the staged selection. Fate does not use the main action.
  • The defender responds first, then players alternate without a fixed window limit. Playing Fate resets the pass count; two consecutive passes resolve the spell or Enhancement. No eligible Fate means an automatic pass. A player who passed can respond again after another Fate is played. Fortune gives the spell or Enhancement caster +4; Misfortune gives the defender −4. Either player may choose +2 or −2 for Twist. Roles stay fixed, and the original dice are never rerolled.
  • Enhancement costs 0 Mana and uses the main action. Roll 2d6, then the opponent responds with Fate first. Alternate responses until both pass consecutively. The final Modified Roll determines the award: 6–8 adds 10 percentage points, 9–11 adds 20, and 12+ adds 30. Use only the highest qualifying tier, capped at 30% total. Below 6 gains nothing. Failure still uses the action. The bonus is banked until your next outgoing damaging spell resolves, then resets to 0%, even if Shield absorbs it all. Non-damaging spells, Fate and self-damage preserve it. Loaded is not added or consumed by this roll, and it grants no Embers/Flow. Played Fate slabs go to discard normally.
  • Aura costs 1 Mana and uses the main action, giving a fixed 10% damage bonus for the next three owner turns; activation does not count. It rolls no dice, opens no Fate window and leaves Loaded unused. Aura cannot stack or be recast while active; it can be used again after expiry. Its countdown starts decreasing after activation, only when you actually pass one of those next three turns, including a confirmed refresh. Aura and Enhancement add together, up to 40%, on total outgoing spell damage including Flow/Embers. Round once before Shield, nearest whole number with halves up. Neither boosts healing, resources, Shield gains/removal or self-damage. Damage consumes Enhancement; Aura lasts until expiry.
  • Opponent damage consumes Shield before HP. Self-damage bypasses your Shield. Healing stops at 100 HP, and Shield gains stop at 30. Reaching 0 HP ends the duel.
  • Fire Embers are capped at 5. Only printed spell effects grant Embers; casting a Fire slab does not grant an automatic Ember. Spending removes the chosen Embers before resolution.
  • Water spells use their existing Flow for effects, damage, and healing. After resolving, gain 1 Flow; a printed additional Flow adds to this gain. Flow is capped at 5. Fate slabs grant no automatic Flow.
  • A Loaded bonus earned this turn is queued for your next own turn. It applies once to your first spell roll in that turn; if unused, it expires when that turn ends. Enhancement and Aura leave it unused.
  • End Turn turns red and reveals Hand Refresh. Click the red End Turn to pass without refreshing.
  • Click Hand Refresh to turn it red and select any number of slabs (including zero). Click it again to discard those selections, draw up to six, and pass immediately. Or click the red End Turn to pass without applying the refresh selections.
Fate sequence: spell roll → defender → caster → defender → caster → … until two consecutive passes. Play one Fate slab per response. Shared-screen games use handoffs; two-screen games let each player respond on their own device. Empty eligible Fate hands pass automatically. Both wizards' Enhancement percentages and Aura countdowns appear on the left of the hand frame. The active wizard's Enhancement button sits on the right, with green Aura below. After a primary spell, Enhancement or Aura, all main actions disable; end or refresh your turn.

Build decks from Home: 1–30 slabs, no per-spell copy limit, and Fire or Water plus Fate. New deck slots start empty and need at least one slab to play. Save deck stores your battle deck on this device without an account. Upload deck loads a JSON draft for review, and Download deck exports a playable deck. The collection has 31 spells from the November 4 spellbook plus Fire-0 and Fire-15, all with larger live compact rules and bold roll thresholds. Canonical effects are unchanged. Fate retains its previous 0 Mana cost because the PDF leaves it unspecified. Select a slab and choose Read slab for full text. Click Spell Tower to review this battle’s composition.
