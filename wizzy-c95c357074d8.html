THE WIZARD’S HANDBOOK

Choose your element

Share one screen locally, or choose Play on two screens to create or join a hosted room. Each wizard has their own hand, spell tower, and discard pile.

  • Start with 100 HP, 3 Mana, and up to 5 spell slabs, drawing as many as your deck contains. Decks may contain 1–30 slabs. Caps are 100 HP, 10 Mana, and 30 Shield. Shield, Embers/Flow, Loaded and Enhancement start at zero, with no active Aura.
  • Each wizard rolls one opening die. The higher roll takes the first turn; ties make both wizards roll again. Opening rolls use no Fate or Loaded bonuses.
  • The wizard going second draws one extra slab at the beginning of their first turn if their tower has a slab, normally starting that turn with 6 rather than 5. Slabs used for defending Fate before that turn remain spent. This opening draw happens once and grants no extra mana.
  • Both wizards take one turn per round. Each gains 2 Mana at the start of their own turn after their opening turn. Unspent Mana carries over up to 10.
  • After each wizard’s opening turn, draw up to 6 slabs at the beginning of their turn. Draws stop when the tower is empty. A full hand uses three slabs on each row.
  • Use one main action per own turn: cast a primary spell, attempt Enhancement, or activate Aura. Fire, Water and Earth slabs are primary spells. Cast Spell pays the printed Mana cost. Damaging Fire spells consume stored Embers when their damage resolves, before adding their new Ember gains.
  • The 10 Fire/Water spells and Earth-1 through Earth-4 roll 2d6 before Fate. Earth-5 resolves immediately without dice or Fate. Blaze costs 0 Mana, gains 1 Ember on resolution, and gains 1 Mana on a final 6+. Swell costs 0 Mana and gains 1 Mana on cast before dice, capped at 10. Its final 7+ gains 1 extra Flow after resolution alongside the normal Water passive: 1 Flow total below 7, 2 total on 7+, capped at 5. Fate changes the extra Flow, not the earned Mana. Both use the primary slot and eligible Loaded. Select one eligible Fate slab, then Cast Fate plays it and gives the other player a response. Skip Fate passes without playing the staged selection. Fate does not use the main action.
  • The defender responds first, then players alternate without a fixed window limit. Playing Fate resets the pass count; two consecutive passes resolve the spell or Enhancement. No eligible Fate means an automatic pass. A player who passed can respond again after another Fate is played. Fortune gives the spell or Enhancement caster +4; Misfortune gives the defender −4. Either player may choose +2 or −2 for Twist. Roles stay fixed, and the original dice are never rerolled.
  • Enhancement costs 0 Mana and uses the main action. Roll 2d6, then the opponent responds with Fate first. Alternate responses until both pass consecutively. The final Modified Roll determines the award: 6–8 adds 10 percentage points, 9–11 adds 20, and 12+ adds 30. Use only the highest qualifying tier, capped at 30% total. Below 6 gains nothing. Failure still uses the action. The bonus is banked until your next outgoing damaging spell resolves, then resets to 0%, even if Shield absorbs it all. Non-damaging spells, Fate and self-damage preserve it. Loaded is not added or consumed by this roll, and it grants no Embers/Flow. Played Fate slabs go to discard normally.
  • Aura costs 1 Mana and uses the main action, giving a fixed 10% damage bonus for the next three owner turns; activation does not count. It rolls no dice, opens no Fate window and leaves Loaded unused. Aura cannot stack or be recast while active; it can be used again after expiry. Its countdown starts decreasing after activation, only when you actually pass one of those next three turns, including a confirmed refresh. Aura and Enhancement add together, up to 40%, on outgoing spell damage including printed Flow damage. Fire’s held-Ember bonus adds to these percentages, up to 65% total. Round once before Shield, nearest whole number with halves up. These damage bonuses do not boost healing, resources, Shield gains/removal or self-damage. Damage consumes Enhancement; Aura lasts until expiry.
  • Opponent damage consumes Shield before HP. Self-damage bypasses your Shield. Healing stops at 100 HP, and Shield gains stop at 30. Reaching 0 HP ends the duel.
  • Fire Embers are capped at 5. Each held Ember adds 5% Fire spell damage, up to 25%. A damaging Fire spell consumes that bank at resolution, even if Shield absorbs all damage, then adds any printed new Ember gains for later attacks. Non-damaging turns preserve Embers. Charred Blade deals 20 base damage; on 7+, deal 30 instead and gain 2 Embers. Sunsetter deals 55 base damage, or 65 on 8+, and uses the same Ember percentage rule.
  • Earth transforms into Golem at 20 Shield. Shield loss does not immediately revert it. At Earth’s actual turn pass, expire existing Golem protection if due, then check Shield: an unprotected Golem at 19 or less reverts before attacking. If Golem remains active, deal a flat 15 damage through normal Shield. This hit is unaffected by Enhancement or Aura and preserves Enhancement. Earth-3 costs 2 Mana: deal 5 damage, and on a final 7+, gain 12 Shield. Earth-4 costs 4 Mana and gains 3 Shield. On a final 6+ after dice and Fate, all damage Earth receives during the opponent’s next turn bypasses Earth’s Shield and hits HP, preserving Shield. This lasts through that opponent’s end-turn effects, then expires. It grants no Mana or new Golem protection. Earth-5’s cast damage is separate and uses ordinary spell bonuses.
  • Water spells use their existing Flow for effects, damage, and healing. After resolving, gain 1 Flow; a printed additional Flow adds to this gain. Flow is capped at 5. Fate slabs grant no automatic Flow.
  • A Loaded bonus earned this turn is queued for your next own turn. It applies once to your first spell roll in that turn; if unused, it expires only when you actually pass that turn. Opening or canceling End Turn/refresh preserves it. Enhancement and Aura leave it unused.
  • End Turn turns red and reveals Hand Refresh. Click the red End Turn to pass without refreshing. With no dialog open, click or tap noninteractive battle background, including the outer gutters, to cancel the unconfirmed drawer or refresh selections. Background taps also clear an uncommitted primary/Fate selection; clearing Fate does not pass.
  • Click Hand Refresh to turn it red and select any number of slabs (including zero). Click it again to discard those selections, draw up to six, and pass immediately. Or click the red End Turn to pass without applying the refresh selections. Cards and controls keep their own actions. Cast Spell, Cast Fate, Skip Fate and confirmed refreshes/passes commit immediately and cannot be undone. Canceling staging keeps any already-used main action spent; Aura counts down only on an actual pass.
Fate sequence: spell/Enhancement roll → defender → caster → defender → caster → … until two consecutive passes. Play one Fate slab per response. Shared-screen games use handoffs; two-screen games let each player respond on their own device. Empty eligible Fate hands pass automatically. Both wizards' Enhancement percentages and Aura countdowns appear on the left of the hand frame. The active wizard's Enhancement button sits on the right, with green Aura below. After a primary spell, Enhancement or Aura, all main actions disable; end or refresh your turn.

Build decks from Home: 1–30 slabs, no per-spell copy limit, and Fire, Water or Earth plus Fate. New deck slots start empty and need at least one slab to play. Save deck stores your battle deck on this device without an account. Upload deck loads a JSON draft for review, and Download deck exports a playable deck. The active collection has 18 spells: five Fire, five Water, five Earth and three Fate. All have larger live compact rules and bold roll thresholds. Spell IDs, saved decks and save/book compatibility are retained. Fate retains its previous 0 Mana cost because the PDF leaves it unspecified. Select a slab and choose Read slab for full text. Click Spell Tower to review this battle’s composition. Storybook's beige pixel-paper view shows only Mainquest labels and boss names, leaving unassigned bosses blank. General opening shows all districts closed; leaving resets them, and a grey map marker opens only its own district. Home's approved rough pixel frames and controls keep the same map geography and district IDs. GPS gating, quest completion and rewards are not connected.
